# RegisterGravity: a Lean 4 check of *Gravitation from Hilbert-Space Granularity* (v11.2)

This project machine-checks the mathematics of the paper in three layers: the count of the horizon that the postulates force, the derivation of every law the paper states from that count, and every number the paper computes from its inputs. The physics the paper takes from outside its postulates is never an axiom here. Each input is a named hypothesis in the statement of the theorem that uses it, so a theorem's statement is its own ledger. For all 168 main theorems, `#print axioms` returns only the logical minimum of classical mathematics, `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` (file `ledger_output.txt`). No proof is left unfinished and none relies on the compiler's evaluation; either would put an extra axiom in the ledger.

Build: Lean `v4.35.0-rc2`, Mathlib commit `045acef0f761280401116e3801cb78ed1b2e716b` (23 September 2026). With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed, run `lake exe cache get` in this folder to download Mathlib's compiled files, then `lake build`, which compiles every module without errors or warnings; `lake env lean RegisterGravity/Ledger.lean` prints the ledger. The workflow in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` does both on GitHub after every change, and it fails on any warning and on any main theorem that depends on an axiom beyond the three.

## What each file proves

`Horizon.lean` formalizes Sec. II, "The count". A `Horizon L` is a finite set of cells with an opposite map and largest rings of `L` cells, subject to exactly what the paper's argument uses: the four clauses of Postulate 2, the last of them (no ring holds every cell) as the existence of a second largest ring. From these, for `L ≥ 4`, it follows the paper step by step. It builds a largest ring that misses a given cell as the text does (`exists_loop_not_mem`), counts `ρ = L/2` rings through every cell from the pairs of that ring (`two_mul_card_through`), and proves Eq. (counts): `2C = L² − 2L + 4` cells, `4R = L² − 2L + 4` rings, and `C = 2R` (`counts`).

`Projective.lean` proves the sentence that follows Eq. (counts): with a cell and its opposite as a point and a largest ring as a line, the register is a finite projective plane in Mathlib's sense (`Configuration.ProjectivePlane`), for `L ≥ 6`, and its order `q` satisfies `2(q + 1) = L`, so `q = L/2 − 1`, with `R = q² + q + 1` points and as many lines. The paper then cites Bruck and Ryser's theorem for the orders such a plane can have; it is a citation, and no derivation uses it.

`Dimension.lean` proves the sentences of Sec. II that follow Eq. (ident): space has three dimensions. A great circle of the sphere about a point is the sphere's intersection with a plane through that point, a two-dimensional subspace of the space, and two great circles meet exactly when their planes share a line. From Mathlib's dimension formula, `finrank (P ⊔ Q) + finrank (P ⊓ Q) = finrank P + finrank Q`: in a space of dimension at most 3 any two planes through the origin share a line (`planes_meet`); in dimension 4 or more two planes can share only the origin (`disjoint_planes_exist`); so if any two planes share a line and there are two distinct planes, the dimension is exactly 3 (`dimension_three`). The hypotheses are the paper's premises as stated: Postulate 2's incidence clause, read on every great circle by the symmetry of space, and "no ring holds every cell". The symmetry of space is a premise, named in the paper's Data availability; it says in which directions a largest ring can run, not where the register's cells lie, and the count does not put them on the sphere.

`RouteB.lean` formalizes the paragraphs of Sec. IV.C on where the strings hold their entropy. The largest rings carry their cyclic order, positions in `ZMod L` one step per cell, with the opposite of a cell `L/2` steps on. From this it proves that `L` is even, the paper's reason (`even_of_halfway`); that at each distance from the body there are `L` cells, two on each ring through the body (`card_dist_eq`); that the cells of an opposite pair lie at the distances `d` and `L/2 − d` (`dist_opp`); that every largest ring has half its cells inside the horizon `L/4` cells out, counting a cell on it as half (`horizon_weight_ring`); that the other rings through a cell of the sphere hold `L − 2` cells at each distance (`per_distance_count`); and that the fraction of their cells inside the horizon that lie within the sphere is exactly `(4n + 2)/L = (4n/L)(1 + 1/2n)` (`route_B_fraction`), which is `4n/L` to leading order in `1/n`.

`Bridge.lean` joins these counts to the chain, which works with real numbers. For a register whose `L` is that of a `Horizon L`, the rings and cells of the horizon number the chain's `L²/4` and `L²/2` times `1 − 2/L + 4/L²` (`counts_exact`). And for a cell of the sphere at distance `n` from the body, the chain's entropy inside the sphere of radius `r = nℓ_c`, `S_in(r) = (4n/L) N_s(r) ln 2`, is the fraction of `route_B_fraction` without its correction `1 + 1/2n`, times the sphere's `N_s(r)` bits (`Sin_from_count`). So Eq. (volume) is the count of Sec. IV.C to leading order in `1/n`.

`Capacity.lean` formalizes the bound of Sec. IV.A and the remarks of Sec. V. When a state is read jointly, the cells on which the other qubits show an outcome that leaves the last qubit in one state form a block of the last string, so outcomes that leave it in the same state share a block and different states have disjoint blocks (`disjoint_blocks`); a product state has one block, the whole string (`product_state`). A block whose fraction of 1s lies strictly between 0 and 1 needs two cells (`two_le_card_of_frac`). A random state of `N` qubits, whose `2^(N−1)` outcomes of the other qubits leave the last in different states, therefore needs `2^N ≤ L` (`ceiling`); if each block needs `r` cells, `2^(N−1) r ≤ L` (`ceiling_soft`); a block of one cell has the fraction 0 or 1 (`one_cell_block`); and Palmer's bit count holds exactly up to the first `N` at which it fails (`palmer_threshold`).

`Chain.lean`, `Galactic.lean`, `DeSitter.lean` and `Cosmo.lean` carry the derivations of Secs. II–V over a structure `Reg` that holds `c`, `ℏ`, `k_B`, `ℓ_c`, `L`, `π` and `ln 2` as positive reals whose values the derivations never use. The register's own quantities are definitions in Lean's sense, not inputs: the units of the cell, the radius of the largest ring `R_Λ = Lℓ_c/2π` (Eq. (ident); the horizon's radius by Sec. IV.D), the counts `L²/2` and `L²/4` (to leading order in `1/L`; `Bridge.counts_exact` gives the exact counts in these terms), and the entropy of one bit per ring, `(L²/4) ln 2`: the horizon holds one string on each largest ring, the most the register allows, since a ring has no more than one bit per cell (the glossary of Sec. II), and one bit is the entropy of a doublet weighted equally, the most a doublet can hold (`one_bit`). The inputs from outside the postulates are hypotheses, listed in the next section.

`Numerics.lean` recomputes every number the paper quotes from the Appendix's inputs, stated as exact rationals, with Mathlib's `π` and `ln 2`, and certifies each by an interval that the quoted value rounds to. It also builds the register with those inputs and discharges the hypotheses of the chain: Jacobson's relation with the register's entropy density returns the measured `G` (`reg_jacobson`), the radius of the largest ring is the observed de Sitter radius (`reg_RL`), and the observed `Λ` meets the boundary condition of Sec. IV.D (`reg_deSitter`). These hold by construction, since the cell is fixed from `G` and `L` from the horizon; what they show is that the hypotheses of the chain can be met together by the observed universe, so that the chain's quantities for these inputs are the certified numbers (the theorems `reg_*`).

`Models.lean` shows that the axioms are consistent, so that no theorem holds vacuously. The Fano plane with each point doubled into a cell and its opposite is a `Horizon 6`, with 14 cells, 7 rings and order 2, and its rings carry positions in the sense of `RouteB.lean`; every axiom is checked by `decide`.

`Ledger.lean` prints the axioms of every main theorem.

## The inputs, and where they enter

Beyond its two postulates the paper takes two inputs, thermodynamics and, at the galactic scale only, elasticity, both in form and neither as a number. Lean shows where each enters.

Nothing else is assumed. Two definitions carry a reading of the paper rather than a count, and they are named here so that the ledger is complete: `Ns`, the strings a sphere holds, carries Jacobson's proportionality of entropy to area, and `Sin`, the entropy a sphere's strings hold inside it, carries the reading of Postulate 1 by which a string's one bit is spread evenly over its cells (`string_share`, `Sin_share`), while its share `4n/L` is the count's (`Sin_from_count`). The count fixes the entropy per unit area, `η = πk_B ln 2/4ℓ_c²` (`eta_eq`), and with `S = ηA` the counts of every sphere in the bulk follow: `bulk_counts` gives Eq. (N), and `ratio_of_areas` shows that a sphere of radius `r` holds the fraction `(r/R_Λ)²` of the horizon's rings and cells. `quarter_any_count` shows that Bekenstein and Hawking's quarter follows from Jacobson's coefficient and the measured `G` for any count, and `cell_from_G` that what the count fixes is the cell, `ℓ_c = √(π ln 2) ℓ_P`.

## The solution of Einstein's equation

Jacobson's argument returns Einstein's equation. Of its classical results the paper uses one solution, Schwarzschild–de Sitter about a mass, `f(r) = 1 − 2GM/c²r − Λr²/3`, and takes it as already proven [F. Kottler, Ann. Phys. (Leipzig) 361, 401 (1918)]; here it is the definition `fSdS`. The field is read from it in the classical weak-field way, `g = (c²/2) df/dr`. The perihelion and deflection formulas of Sec. IV.B are classical results as well, and `solar_tests` evaluates them. Everything else the paper takes from general relativity is derived from the solution.

The field is `g = GM/r² − Λc²r/3` (`gSdS_eq`). With the mass alone it is Newton's law, and with `I1` it gives Padmanabhan's equipartition, `Mc² = 2N_s(r) k_BT_a ln 2`, two Landauer energies per string (`newton`, `equipartition_in_cells`), where `Mc²` is the Komar energy of the solution (`komar_mass`); Gauss's law follows from the same field (`gauss`). Without the mass the solution has its horizon at `R_Λ` exactly when `Λ = 3/R_Λ²` (`deSitter`). In Einstein's equation `Λ` is an integration constant, and this condition, the hypothesis `hdS` of the theorems that use `Λ`, fixes it; Sec. IV.D derives it from Postulate 1 and the solution's horizon, given `Λ > 0`, and so does `horizon_radius` (below). Its content is Eq. (energy): with `hdS` the field of the solution without a mass is `−c²/R_Λ` at the horizon, so the horizon's surface gravity `a_Λ = c²/R_Λ` is derived; the ring of an observer held at that acceleration is the largest ring, `L_{a_Λ} = L`, so the period of the horizon, `2πc/a_Λ`, is the lap time `Lt_c` of the largest ring (`horizon_period`), and with `I1` its temperature is Unruh's at `a_Λ`, which is `E_c/L = ℏc/2πR_Λ`, the Gibbons–Hawking temperature (`horizon_temperature`). The proper distance from the observer to that horizon is `πR_Λ/2 = Lℓ_c/4`, a quarter of a largest ring (`proper_distance`). With the mass, the outer horizon lies within `2ε²/R_Λ` of `R_Λ − ε`, with `ε = GM/c²`, and there is no horizon farther out (`sds_horizon_shift`, `sds_outer_horizon`). A sphere about the mass gains area with geodesic distance more slowly than in flat space, by a rate between `8πGM/c²` and `8πGM/c²√f` (`area_deficit`). Summed from the body's surface `b` out to `r` (`shortfall_sum`), the shortfall lies between `8πGM(r − b)/c²` and that over `√f` at the surface, and at one bit per ring (`removed_rings`) the entropy it removes lies between `S_M(r − b)` and `S_M(r − b)/√f`: to first order, far outside the body, it is `S_M(r) = 2πnα` (`shortfall_removes`). The field with the boundary condition is the pull less the push (`push`); the zero-gravity radius lies below the crossing for any mass below `c²R_Λ/π³G` (`zero_gravity`, `zero_gravity_mass`), and the push meets the deep pull at `r = R_Λv_c/c` (`crossover`, `crossover_speed`). At the horizon the Misner–Sharp energy is `E_Λ` and the Komar energy is `−2E_Λ` (`komar_misner_sharp`). The drift of `G` that a Hubble-radius horizon would give, `Ġ = 2H(1 + q)G`, is computed by differentiation (`G_drift`); with `L` fixed, `GΛ` is constant and `Λ̇/Λ = −Ġ/G` (`constancy_of_Lambda`); and the fluid equation, `ρ̇ = −3H(1 + w)ρ`, follows from the first law (`fluid_equation`). Since the ring's density `3π²ρ_c ln 2/2L²` does not change, it gives `w = −1` exactly (`w_exact`).

Some hypotheses are not inputs. `hdS`, that the horizon of the solution without a mass is at `R_Λ`, is derived by `horizon_radius` (Sec. IV.D) from Postulate 1 and the solution's horizon, given a horizon at all (`hΛ`, `Λ > 0`, which the paper derives from the rings through Einstein's equations and the files take as a hypothesis): a largest ring keeps its length, so it is at rest relative to its center, and nothing beyond the horizon is at rest, so its radius lies within the horizon (`hrest`, `f(R_Λ) ≥ 0`); and the horizon's great circles are closed lines of translation, so they are rings, and no ring has more than `L` cells, so the horizon's circumference is at most `Lℓ_c` (`P1`); the theorems that use `Λ` keep `hdS` as their hypothesis for brevity. A few theorems take the condition that defines the quantity they treat: the proper distance, with `s(0) = 0` and `ds/dr = 1/√f` (`proper_distance`; `proper_distance_exists` shows that `R_Λ arcsin(r/R_Λ)` meets it); the shortfall of a sphere's area, by its rate per unit of geodesic distance (`shortfall_removes`); the vanishing of the field at `r_z` (`zero_gravity`); the balance of the deep pull and the push (`crossover`); the flat rotation curve, `r g_D → v_c²` (`deep_regime_upper_bound`); a random state, every joint outcome of the other qubits leaving the last in a different state, with a probability strictly between 0 and 1 (`ceiling`); and the observed bound on `|ρ̇/ρ|` (`w_bound`). `temperature_led` takes as its premise the alternative that Sec. IV.C rejects, that gravity follows the excess of the Deser–Levin temperature over the floor, and derives the coefficient that alternative would give.

## What was checked, and what it found

Every relation of the chain holds exactly as v11.2 writes it, and every number it computes is certified: each theorem gives an interval every point of which rounds to the value the paper quotes. Observational values and their uncertainties are inputs, not claims. Checking the draft of v11.2 found one slip. Appendix A gave `log₂ L_cos = 204.77`, which is the logarithm of `L_cos` after rounding it to `4.37×10⁶¹`. The logarithm of `L_cos` itself is `204.7648`, and `log2_Lcos` certifies `204.755 < log₂ L_cos < 204.765`, so v11.2 says `204.76`.

Since v11.1, the formalization follows the paper's move to one bit per ring: the entropy is `(L²/4) ln 2`, `ln 2` is a field of `Reg`, and every formula that carries it (`η`, `G`, `ℓ_c`, the shift of the horizon, the crossing, `V_0`, the slope of `V_M`, the apparent mass, `GΛ`, `E_Λ` and `ρ_Λ`) is derived again and its numbers certified again. `π` and `ln 2` are now Mathlib's, with Mathlib's own bounds, where v11.1 took the bounds on `π` as a hypothesis, and the projective plane uses Mathlib's `Combinatorics.Configuration` in place of an extract of it. New in v11.2 are the bridges that join the count to the chain (the exact counts and Eq. (volume)), the shortfall summed into the entropy a mass removes, the paper's construction of a ring that misses a cell, the evenness of `L` from the opposite lying halfway round, the exact count behind the fraction `4n/L` and the count at each distance, the capacity count and its soft form, Palmer's bit count for every `N`, the proper distance to the horizon, the outer horizon with a mass, the deep-regime bound, the temperature floor with Deser–Levin, the Misner–Sharp and Komar energies, the coefficient of Einstein's equation, one bit as the entropy of a doublet, the ratio of the Planck density to `ρ_Λ`, `Λ̇/Λ = −Ġ/G`, and the numbers that go with them. The zero-velocity radii of v11.1 (111 pc and 1.6 Mpc) are gone, as the paper no longer quotes them, and so are the checks of two v11 misprints that v11.1 corrected.
