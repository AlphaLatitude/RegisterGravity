# RegisterGravity: a Lean 4 check of *Gravitation from Hilbert-Space Granularity* (v11.1)

This project machine-checks the mathematics of the paper in three layers: the count of the horizon that Postulate 2 forces, the chain from that count to every law the paper states, and every number the paper computes from its inputs. The physics the paper takes from outside its postulates is never an axiom here. Each input is a named hypothesis in the statement of the theorem that uses it, so a theorem's statement is its own ledger, and `#print axioms` for all 106 main theorems returns only the logical minimum, `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` (file `ledger_output.txt`). There is no `sorry` and no `native_decide`.

Build: Lean `v4.35.0-rc2`, Mathlib commit `045acef0f761280401116e3801cb78ed1b2e716b` (23 September 2026). With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed, run `lake exe cache get` in this folder to download Mathlib's compiled files, then `lake build`, which compiles the nine modules without errors or warnings; `lake env lean RegisterGravity/Ledger.lean` prints the ledger. The workflow in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` does both on GitHub after every change and fails if any main theorem depends on an axiom beyond the three.

## What each file proves

`Horizon.lean` formalizes Sec. II, "The count". A `Horizon L` is a finite set of cells with an opposite map and largest rings of `L` cells, subject to exactly what the paper's argument uses: both clauses of Postulate 2, the joint-reading clause of Postulate 1 (any two largest rings share a cell), and that the horizon is more than one ring. From these, for `L ≥ 4`, it proves Eq. (counts): `ρ = L/2` rings through every cell, `2C = L² − 2L + 4` cells, `4R = L² − 2L + 4` rings, and `C = 2R`. That `L` is even is a consequence, not an assumption.

`Projective.lean` proves the sentence that follows Eq. (counts): with a cell and its opposite as a point and a largest ring as a line, the horizon is a finite projective plane in Mathlib's sense (`Configuration.ProjectivePlane`), for `L ≥ 6`, and its order `q` satisfies `2(q + 1) = L`, so `q = L/2 − 1`, with `R = q² + q + 1` points and as many lines. `Configuration.lean` is a verbatim extract of Mathlib's `Combinatorics/Configuration.lean`, without its matrix section, so that the build needs a smaller part of Mathlib.

`Models.lean` shows that the axioms are consistent, so no theorem holds vacuously: the Fano plane with each point doubled into a cell and its opposite is a `Horizon 6`, with 14 cells, 7 rings, and order 2, every axiom checked by `decide`.

`Chain.lean`, `Galactic.lean` and `Cosmo.lean` carry the chain of Secs. II–V over a structure `Reg` holding `c, ℏ, k_B, ℓ_c, L, π`. The register's own inputs are definitions: the units of the cell, the placement `R_Λ = Lℓ_c/2π`, and the counts `L²/2` and `L²/4`. The inputs from outside the postulates are hypotheses, and the one solution of Einstein's equation that the paper uses is a definition from which everything else it takes from general relativity is derived.

`Numerics.lean` recomputes every quoted number from the Appendix's inputs, stated as exact rationals, and certifies each by an interval that the quoted value rounds to. It proves the capacity condition of Eq. (nmax): a state of `N` qubits spread over all `2^N` outcomes gives each a frequency of at least one of the string's `L` bits, so `2^N ≤ L` (`capacity`), and `N_max = 204` for both pins (`nmax_cos`, `nmax_gal`). It also builds the register with those inputs and shows that Jacobson's relation with the register's entropy density returns the measured `G`, which discharges the hypothesis `hJ` of the chain, that the placement returns the observed de Sitter radius, and that the observed `Λ` meets the boundary condition `hdS` of the chain.

## The inputs, and where they enter

Beyond its two postulates the paper takes two inputs, thermodynamics and, at the galactic scale only, elasticity, both in form and neither as a number. Lean shows where each enters.

| Kind | Input | In the code | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thermodynamics | a horizon is thermal, `k_BT = ℏ/τ` | `I1` | `T_dS`, `T_Unruh`, `newton`, `entropy_removed` |
| Thermodynamics | Jacobson: a horizon's entropy is proportional to its area, `S = ηA`, and heat crossing a local horizon obeys the Clausius relation, which gives Einstein's equation with `G = c³k_B/4ℏη` | `hJ`; `Ns` | `G_eq`, `cell_from_G`, `entropy_quarter`, `G_mul_eta`, `newton`, `bulk_counts`, `ratio_of_areas`, `shift_in_cells`, `removed_rings`, `crossing`, `elastic`, `apparent_mass`, `tully_fisher`, `G_Lambda`, `EΛ_eq`, `ρΛ_eq`, `push`, `zero_velocity` |
| Thermodynamics | the first law for a component of the universe, `d(ρV) = −p dV` | `first_law` | `fluid_equation`, `w_exact`, `w_bound` |
| Elasticity, galactic only | Verlinde's elastic response, `ε²A = dV_M/dr` and `Σ_D = (a_Λ/8πG)ε` | `I5`, `I6` | `elastic`, `apparent_mass`, `tully_fisher` |

Nothing else is assumed. The count fixes the entropy per unit area, `η = πk_B/4ℓ_c²`, and with `S = ηA` the counts of every sphere in the bulk follow: `bulk_counts` gives Eq. (N), and `ratio_of_areas` shows that a sphere of radius `r` holds the fraction `(r/R_Λ)²` of the horizon's rings and cells. The share of a string's entropy inside a sphere follows from Postulate 1: its one unit is spread evenly over its cells, and of the `L/4` between the body and the horizon, `n` lie inside the sphere (`string_share`, `Sin_share`).

## The solution of Einstein's equation

Jacobson's argument returns Einstein's equation, and the paper uses one of its solutions, Schwarzschild–de Sitter about a mass, `f(r) = 1 − 2GM/c²r − Λr²/3` (`fSdS`). This is the one result of general relativity stated rather than proved: that it solves Einstein's equation is a computation of curvature, and Mathlib has no Lorentzian curvature. Everything else the paper takes from general relativity is derived from it.

The field of the solution, `g = (c²/2) df/dr = GM/r² − Λc²r/3`, is computed by differentiation (`gSdS_eq`). With the mass alone it is Newton's law, and with `I1` it gives Padmanabhan's equipartition, `Mc² = 2N_s(r) k_BT_a` (`newton`); Gauss's law, `Σ = g/4πG`, follows from the same field (`gauss`). Without the mass the solution has its horizon at `R_Λ` exactly when `Λ = 3/R_Λ²` (`deSitter`). In Einstein's equation `Λ` is an integration constant, and this boundary condition, the hypothesis `hdS` of the theorems that use `Λ`, fixes it, as Sec. IV.D says. With the mass, the horizon is drawn in by `GM/c²` (`sds_horizon_shift`). A sphere about the mass gains area with geodesic distance more slowly than in flat space, by a rate between `8πGM/c²` and `8πGM/c²√f` (`area_deficit`), so the sphere at geodesic distance `r` has lost `8πGMr/c²` to first order in `GM/c²r`, which is `S_M(r) = 2πnα` rings (`removed_rings`). The field with the boundary condition is the pull less the push (`push`); it vanishes at the zero-velocity radius (`zero_velocity`), and the push meets the deep pull at the crossover (`crossover`). The drift of `G` that a Hubble-radius horizon would give, `Ġ = 2H(1 + q)G`, is computed by differentiation (`G_drift`), and the fluid equation, `ρ̇ = −3H(1 + w)ρ`, follows from the first law (`fluid_equation`); since the ring's density `3π²ρ_c/2L²` does not change, it gives `w = −1` exactly (`w_exact`).

Three kinds of hypothesis remain that are not inputs. `hdS` is the boundary condition above. A few theorems take the condition that defines the radius or the quantity they treat: the vanishing of the field at `r_z` (`zero_velocity`), the balance of the deep pull and the push (`crossover`), and the observed bound on `|ρ̇/ρ|` (`w_bound`). And `temperature_led` takes as its premise the alternative that Sec. IV.C rejects, that gravity follows the excess of the Deser–Levin temperature over the floor, and derives the coefficient it would give.

Everything else in the chain uses the register's definitions alone: the shares of area `2ℓ_c²/π` and `4ℓ_c²/π`, the lap time, the quarter lap, Eq. (N), `η = πk_B/4ℓ_c²`, the temperature floor `a ≥ c²/R_Λ`, `S_M(r) = 2πnα`, Eq. (volume), `V_0 = Lℓ_c³/3π`, `a_M = πc²/12R_Λ = (π²/6)c²/Lℓ_c` against Verlinde's `c²/6R_Λ`, the pin `L_gal`, `L_gal/L_cos = a_M/a_0`, and the Hubble rate.

## What was checked, and what it found

Every relation of the chain holds exactly as v11.1 writes it, and every number it computes is certified: each theorem gives an interval every point of which rounds to the value the paper quotes. Observational values and their uncertainties are inputs, not claims. Checking v11 found three slips, all corrected in v11.1, and one point on what the count needs.

1. **A misprint in Sec. III.C.** Version 11 wrote `L²/4 = πR_Λ²/ℓ_c² = A_hor/4ℓ_P²`. `entropy_misprint` proves that `L²/4 = πR_Λ²/ℓ_c²` holds only if `π = 1`; the correct chain, now in the text, is `L²/4 = π²R_Λ²/ℓ_c² = πR_Λ²/ℓ_P² = A_hor/4ℓ_P²` (`entropy_quarter`).
2. **The Sun's shift in cells.** Version 11's Appendix gave `α/π = 5.16×10³⁷`, which follows from a rounded `M☉ = 1.989×10³⁰ kg`. With the IAU heliocentric constant `GM☉ = 1.32712440018×10²⁰ m³ s⁻²` it is `5.154×10³⁷`; v11.1 states that input and `5.15×10³⁷`. Every other solar number is unaffected.
3. **The crossover radius.** Version 11's Sec. IV.D put the meeting of the deep pull and the push at 4 Mpc for `10¹¹ M☉`; the value is `3.73 Mpc` (`crossover_radius`), and v11.1 says `3.7 Mpc`.
4. **What the count needs.** The count uses the joint-reading clause of Postulate 1 as well as Postulate 2, as the text says, and `L ≥ 4` (`L ≥ 6` for Mathlib's non-degenerate projective plane). Bruck–Ryser is not in Mathlib and stays a citation.

The certified numbers include `ℓ_c = 2.865×10⁻³⁵ m`, `ℓ_P = 1.616×10⁻³⁵ m`, the units of the cell, `H_∞ = 1.81×10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹`, `R_Λ = 1.66×10²⁶ m`, `Λ = 1.09×10⁻⁵² m⁻²`, `L_cos = 3.64×10⁶¹` with `204.495 < log₂ L_cos < 204.505`, `L_gal = 4.30×10⁶¹` with `204.735 < log₂ L_gal < 204.745`, the ceiling `2²⁰⁴ < L < 2²⁰⁵` for both, the ratio `1.18` with `0.24 < log₂(L_gal/L_cos) < 0.25`, Palmer's bit count holding at 211 and failing at 212, with the Appendix's `2²⁰⁴`, `2²⁰⁵`, `2²¹²`, `2²¹³`, `211L` and `212L` to three figures, `L²/2 = 6.6×10¹²²` cells and `L²/4 = 3.3×10¹²²` rings, Davies' `405 < log₂ 10¹²² < 405.5` and `407.99 < log₂(L²/2) < 408.01`, the `G` bracket `3.3×10⁻¹¹` to `1.3×10⁻¹⁰` with the measured `G` inside and within 0.2 percent of its geometric middle, `α☉ = 1.62×10³⁸`, `α☉/π = 5.15×10³⁷`, `Lα☉ = 5.9×10⁹⁹` and `5.9×10¹¹⁰` for `10¹¹ M☉`, `n = 5.22×10⁴⁵` and `5.93×10⁻³ m s⁻²` at 1 au, Mercury's `42.98″` per century and the `1.75″` deflection, the phase grid fifty-four orders below Mercury, `a_M = 1.42×10⁻¹⁰` and Verlinde's `0.90×10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻²`, the 85 percent saturation, `0.9σ` and `2.3σ`, `γ = 1.39` against `π²/6 = 1.645`, `0.22` against `π/12 = 0.26`, the temperature-led `2c²/R_Λ` at nine times `a_0` and its 38 percent transition, the Tully–Fisher 208 km/s, `E_Λ = 1.00×10⁷⁰ J`, `ρ_Λ = 5.25×10⁻¹⁰ J m⁻³`, `GΛ = 7.28×10⁻⁶³`, `k_BT_dS = 3.03×10⁻⁵³ J`, `τ* = 3.5×10¹⁸ s = 1.1×10¹¹ yr`, the push `4.9×10⁻²⁵ m s⁻²` at 1 au, `r_z = 111 pc` and `1.6 Mpc`, the crossover at `3.7 Mpc`, `H(1)/H_0 = 1.79`, `H_0 = 6.89×10⁻¹¹ yr⁻¹`, `|1 + w_0| ≲ 4.8×10⁻⁴`, and the drift `Ġ/G ≈ 6×10⁻¹¹ yr⁻¹`, six hundred times the lunar-laser-ranging bound.

## What Lean does not check

Lean checks mathematics. The steps that turn physics into the hypotheses above are not re-derived: Raychaudhuri and the Clausius relation into Einstein's equation, the thermality of a horizon, the first law, and Verlinde's elastic medium enter as the named hypotheses, and the ledger shows exactly which theorem uses which. The Schwarzschild–de Sitter solution enters as a definition whose derivation from Einstein's equation is not checked. `π` enters the numerics through the bounds `3.141592 < π < 3.141593`, which are Mathlib's `Real.pi_gt_d6` and `Real.pi_lt_d6`, taken as a hypothesis only to keep the build small.
