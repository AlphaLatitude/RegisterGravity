# RegisterGravity: a Lean 4 check of *Gravitation from Hilbert-Space Granularity* (v13)

This project machine-checks the mathematics of the paper in three layers: the count of the horizon that the postulates force, the derivation of every law the paper states from that count, and every number the paper computes from its inputs. The physics the paper takes from outside its postulates is never an axiom here. Each thermodynamic and elastic input is a named hypothesis in the statement of the theorem that uses it, so a theorem's statement is its own ledger; the symmetry of space enters through definitions (a ring's share of area the same everywhere, `ringShare` and `Ns`) and through the solution taken as known, as the inputs section says; and each reading of the postulates (the five identifications of the paper's Table II) is a named definition: the files check what follows from the readings, not the readings, which no formalization can. For all 174 main theorems, `#print axioms` returns only the logical minimum of classical mathematics, `[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]` (file `ledger_output.txt`). No proof is left unfinished and none relies on the compiler's evaluation; either would put an extra axiom in the ledger. This is release v13 of the package, which goes with version 13 of the paper (Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23073991); in the development numbering of the changelog below it is v11.2.11-13.

Build: Lean `v4.35.0-rc2`, Mathlib commit `045acef0f761280401116e3801cb78ed1b2e716b` (23 September 2026). With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed, run `lake exe cache get` in this folder to download Mathlib's compiled files, then `lake build`, which compiles every module without errors or warnings; `lake env lean RegisterGravity/Ledger.lean` prints the ledger. The workflow in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` does both on GitHub after every change, and it fails on any warning and on any main theorem that depends on an axiom beyond the three.

## What each file proves

`Horizon.lean` formalizes Sec. II, "The count". A `Horizon L` is a finite set of cells with an opposite map and largest rings of `L` cells, subject to exactly what the paper's argument uses: the four clauses of Postulate 2, the last of them (no ring holds every cell) as the existence of a second largest ring. From these, for `L ≥ 4`, it follows the paper step by step. It builds a largest ring that misses a given cell as the text does (`exists_loop_not_mem`), counts `ρ = L/2` rings through every cell from the pairs of that ring (`two_mul_card_through`), and proves Eq. (counts): `2C = L² − 2L + 4` cells, `4R = L² − 2L + 4` rings, and `C = 2R` (`counts`).

`Projective.lean` proves the sentence that follows Eq. (counts): with a cell and its opposite as a point and a largest ring as a line, the register is a finite projective plane in Mathlib's sense (`Configuration.ProjectivePlane`), for `L ≥ 6`, and its order `q` satisfies `2(q + 1) = L`, so `q = L/2 − 1`, with `R = q² + q + 1` points and as many lines. The paper then cites Bruck and Ryser's theorem for the orders such a plane can have; it is a citation, and no derivation uses it. No drawing of the register in space is assumed anywhere in the files, and none exists: every line of the plane holds `L/2 ≥ 3` points for `L ≥ 6`, and by the Sylvester–Gallai theorem a finite set of points of the real projective plane, not all on one line, determines a line through exactly two of them, so the register cannot be drawn with its points on a sphere and its lines as great circles (Sec. II, "The horizon's area"). The count is an incidence structure; what joins it to the horizon's area is the reading `Chain.ringShare` (below).

`RouteB.lean` formalizes the paragraphs of Sec. IV.C on where the strings hold their entropy. The largest rings carry their cyclic order, positions in `ZMod L` one step per cell, with the opposite of a cell `L/2` steps on. From this it proves that `L` is even, the paper's reason (`even_of_halfway`); that at each distance from the body there are `L` cells, two on each ring through the body (`card_dist_eq`); that the cells of an opposite pair lie at the distances `d` and `L/2 − d` (`dist_opp`); that every largest ring has half its cells inside the horizon `L/4` cells out, counting a cell on it as half (`horizon_weight_ring`); that the other rings through a cell of the sphere hold `L − 2` cells at each distance (`per_distance_count`); and that the fraction of their cells inside the horizon that lie within the sphere is exactly `(4n + 2)/L = (4n/L)(1 + 1/2n)` (`route_B_fraction`), which is `4n/L` to leading order in `1/n`.

`Bridge.lean` joins these counts to the chain, which works with real numbers. For a register whose `L` is that of a `Horizon L`, the rings and cells of the horizon number the chain's `L²/4` and `L²/2` times `1 − 2/L + 4/L²` (`counts_exact`). And for a cell of the sphere at distance `n` from the body, the chain's entropy inside the sphere of radius `r = nℓ_c`, `S_in(r) = (4n/L) N_s(r) ln 2`, is the fraction of `route_B_fraction` without its correction `1 + 1/2n`, times the sphere's `N_s(r)` bits (`Sin_from_count`). So the share in Eq. (volume) is the count of Sec. IV.C to leading order in `1/n`; the strings it multiplies, `N_s(r)`, are the reading `Ns`, and the coefficient `π/12` is Verlinde's `1/6` normalized where the solution puts the horizon, a quarter lap along a ring, which the register counts as `L/4` cells. The share enters `Galactic.Sin` as `κn/L` with `κ` a parameter, `4` the count's and `2π` Verlinde's flat ball, and the coefficient as `πc²/(3κR_Λ)` (`aM`, `aM_values`), so that what rests on the reading is in view. In its general form, the share by ring distance times the strings by area (`Galactic.SinGen`), the law is Eq. (volume) for `r ≪ R_Λ`, where ring distance and areal radius agree, and at the horizon itself, ring distance `Lℓ_c/4` and area `4πR_Λ²`, it returns the horizon's entropy `(L²/4) ln 2` exactly (`Sin_general`): the two readings agree there.

`Capacity.lean` formalizes the bound of Sec. IV.A and the remarks of Sec. V. When a state is measured jointly, the strings are read together bit by bit, each bit of the last string paired with one outcome of the other qubits (the pairing is the paper's fifth reading of Postulate 1, which is Palmer's, `N` correlated strings with one permutation applied to all, and needs no shared cell), and the cells of the last string whose bits are paired with one joint outcome of the other qubits form a block of the last string, the paper's definition; the files index blocks through a labeling `s` of the outcomes, the paper's blocks being `s` the identity, and blocks of different labels are disjoint (`disjoint_blocks`). A block whose fraction of 1s lies strictly between 0 and 1 needs two cells (`two_le_card_of_frac`). A state of `N` qubits each of whose `2^(N−1)` joint outcomes of the other qubits occurs with the last qubit's bit given it at a probability strictly between 0 and 1 needs `2^(N−1)` blocks of two cells on the last string, so `2^N ≤ L` (`ceiling_by_outcome` with the paper's blocks, `ceiling` with any injective labeling): a random state is such a state, and so is a product state of superposed qubits, so the bound holds for every state in which all `2^N` joint outcomes occur (Sec. IV.A). `product_state` concerns a coarser grouping, blocks by conditional state, under which all outcomes of a product state share one block; its docstring says so. If each block needs `r` cells, `2^(N−1) r ≤ L` (`ceiling_soft`); a block of one cell has the fraction 0 or 1 (`one_cell_block`); and Palmer's bit count holds exactly up to the first `N` at which it fails (`palmer_threshold`).

`Chain.lean`, `Galactic.lean`, `DeSitter.lean` and `Cosmo.lean` carry the derivations of Secs. II–V over a structure `Reg` that holds `c`, `ℏ`, `k_B`, `ℓ_c`, `L`, `π` and `ln 2` as positive reals whose values the derivations never use. The register's own quantities are definitions in Lean's sense, not inputs: the units of the cell, the radius of the largest ring `R_Λ = Lℓ_c/2π` (Eq. (ident); the horizon's radius by Sec. IV.D), the counts `L²/2` and `L²/4` (to leading order in `1/L`; `Bridge.counts_exact` gives the exact counts in these terms), and the entropy of one bit per ring, `(L²/4) ln 2`: every ring carries one string (the reading of Postulate 1 in Sec. II), what can change in a free string on its ring is which way its bits move, a doublet, and one bit is the entropy of a doublet weighted equally, the most a doublet can hold (`one_bit`); interactions enter the count only as corrections. The thermodynamic and elastic inputs from outside the postulates are hypotheses, listed in the next section, where the symmetry of space is also placed.

`Numerics.lean` recomputes every number the paper quotes from the Appendix's inputs, stated as exact rationals, with Mathlib's `π` and `ln 2`, and certifies each by an interval that the quoted value rounds to. It also builds the register with those inputs and discharges the hypotheses of the chain: Jacobson's relation with the register's entropy density returns the measured `G` (`reg_jacobson`), the radius of the largest ring is the observed de Sitter radius (`reg_RL`), and the observed `Λ` meets the boundary condition of Sec. IV.D (`reg_deSitter`). These hold by construction, since the cell is fixed from `G` and `L` from the horizon; what they show is that the hypotheses of the chain can be met together by the observed universe, so that the chain's quantities for these inputs are the certified numbers (the theorems `reg_*`).

`Models.lean` shows that the axioms are consistent, so that no theorem holds vacuously. The Fano plane with each point doubled into a cell and its opposite is a `Horizon 6`, with 14 cells, 7 rings and order 2, and its rings carry positions in the sense of `RouteB.lean`; every axiom is checked by `decide`. It is the only model exhibited: a `Horizon L` exists exactly when a projective plane of order `L/2 − 1` does, and for the paper's `L ≈ 4.4×10⁶¹` (order about `2.2×10⁶¹`) that is not known either way, as Sec. II of the paper says; the theorems about a `Horizon L` are conditional on one existing.

`Ledger.lean` prints the axioms of every main theorem.

## The inputs, and where they enter

Beyond its two postulates the paper takes from outside them, as Sec. III lists, the symmetry of space (Sec. II), thermodynamics (Sec. III), Verlinde's elastic response (Sec. IV.C), and the first law for the cosmic fluid (Sec. VI), none of them a number, and it reads the postulates in five places (Secs. II, IV.A and IV.C). The symmetry of space is folded into `ringShare` and `Ns` (a ring's share of area the same everywhere) and into the solution taken as known; the readings are definitions (below), the fifth a function; the rest are hypotheses, and Lean shows where each enters.

Nothing else is assumed. Four definitions and one function carry a reading of the postulates rather than a count, the five the paper names in Sec. III, so that the ledger is complete. `Ahor` and `ringShare` carry the count identified with the horizon's area: the register is not drawn on the horizon, and what the count and the sphere share is a number, `R` rings for the area `4πR_Λ²`, each ring worth `4ℓ_c²/π` of it (`shares`). `Shor`, the entropy of the horizon, carries the reading of Postulate 1 by which every ring carries one string, with a bit on each of its cells, so that the horizon holds one bit for each of its rings (`one_bit`). `Ns`, the strings of a sphere that is not a horizon, carries the area law of a screen (Sec. II, "Counting in the bulk"; Sec. III uses it on every local horizon, the density the same on each): the register fixes a sphere's incidences, `L²/2` at every distance, so counted by incidence every sphere would hold all `L²/4` strings, as a horizon does, and the register would know no distance; the number of a sphere's strings is read instead from its area, one for each ring's share of it, the same everywhere. `Sin`, the entropy a sphere's strings hold inside it, carries the reading of Postulate 1 by which a string's one bit is spread evenly over its cells inside the horizon, since the bit is which way the whole string moves, a property of no one cell, and every cell is read alike, the arrangement of the bits being no part of the state (Secs. IV.A, IV.C; `string_share`, `Sin_share`), while its share `4n/L` is the count's (`Sin_from_count`). The fifth reading, that a joint measurement reads its strings in step, the `k`-th bit of each together (Sec. IV.A), enters `Capacity.ceiling` as the function `e` that gives, at each cell of the last string, the outcome the other qubits show. The count fixes the entropy per unit area, `η = πk_B ln 2/4ℓ_c²` (`eta_eq`), and with `S = ηA` the counts of every sphere in the bulk follow: `bulk_counts` gives Eq. (N), and `ratio_of_areas` shows that a sphere of radius `r` holds the fraction `(r/R_Λ)²` of the register's rings, every one of which crosses every observer's horizon with half its cells inside. `quarter_any_count` shows that Bekenstein and Hawking's quarter follows from Jacobson's coefficient and the measured `G` for any count, and `cell_from_G` that what the count fixes is the cell, `ℓ_c = √(π ln 2) ℓ_P`.

## The solution of Einstein's equation

Jacobson's argument returns Einstein's equation. Of its classical results the paper uses one solution, Schwarzschild–de Sitter about a mass, `f(r) = 1 − 2GM/c²r − Λr²/3`, and takes it as already proven [F. Kottler, Ann. Phys. (Leipzig) 361, 401 (1918)]; here it is the definition `fSdS`. The field is read from it in the classical weak-field way, `g = (c²/2) df/dr`. The perihelion and deflection formulas of Sec. IV.B are classical results as well, and `solar_tests` evaluates them. The register's rings are the largest rings, of `L` cells, each a great circle of the horizon of some observer at rest (Secs. II, IV.D); every one crosses an observer's horizon with half its cells inside and carries the entropy inside it (`Galactic.Sin`), and the horizon's own entropy is the register's count identified with its area (`Shor`). With a mass inside, the solution's closed geodesics are the great circles of its horizons' throats, of `L_M = L − 2α/ln 2` cells at the cosmological horizon and `4πGM/c²ℓ_c` at a black hole's, the shorter rings Postulate 1 allows, outside the count of Postulate 2; the area law (`Ns`), one bit per ring's share of area, gives the horizon `L_M²/4` bits, the number Eq. (counts) would give with `L_M` in place of `L`, which is `Galactic.entropy_removed`. Everything else the paper takes from general relativity is derived from the solution.

The field is `g = GM/r² − Λc²r/3` (`gSdS_eq`). With the mass alone it is Newton's law, and with `I1` it gives Padmanabhan's equipartition, `Mc² = 2N_s(r) k_BT_a ln 2`, two Landauer energies per string (`newton`, `equipartition_in_cells`), where `Mc²` is the Komar energy of the solution (`komar_mass`); Gauss's law follows from the same field (`gauss`). Without the mass the solution has its horizon at `R_Λ` exactly when `Λ = 3/R_Λ²` (`deSitter`). In Einstein's equation `Λ` is an integration constant, and this condition, the hypothesis `hdS` of the theorems that use `Λ`, fixes it; Sec. IV.D derives it from Postulate 1 and the solution's horizon, given `Λ > 0`, and so does `horizon_radius` (below). Its content is Eq. (energy): with `hdS` the field of the solution without a mass is `−c²/R_Λ` at the horizon, so the horizon's surface gravity `a_Λ = c²/R_Λ` is derived; the period of a horizon of that surface gravity, `2πc/a_Λ`, is `L` ticks, the lap time `Lt_c` of the largest ring (`horizon_period`), and with `I1` its temperature is Unruh's at `a_Λ`, which is `E_c/L = ℏc/2πR_Λ`, the Gibbons–Hawking temperature (`horizon_temperature`). The proper distance from the observer to that horizon is `πR_Λ/2 = Lℓ_c/4`, a quarter of a largest ring (`proper_distance`). With the mass, the outer horizon lies within `2ε²/R_Λ` of `R_Λ − ε`, with `ε = GM/c²`, and there is no horizon farther out (`sds_horizon_shift`, `sds_outer_horizon`). A sphere about the mass gains area with geodesic distance more slowly than in flat space, by a rate between `8πGM/c²` and `8πGM/c²√f` (`area_deficit`). Summed from the body's surface `b` out to `r` (`shortfall_sum`), the shortfall lies between `8πGM(r − b)/c²` and that over `√f` at the surface, and at one bit per ring (`removed_rings`) the entropy it removes lies between `S_M(r − b)` and `S_M(r − b)/√f`: to first order, far outside the body, it is `S_M(r) = 2πnα` (`shortfall_removes`). The elastic response (Sec. IV.C): with the response absent inside the crossing, Verlinde's equality `∫₀^r ε²A dr′ = V_M(r)` fails at every radius up to the crossing, since the integral stays `0` there while `V_M` grows as `r` (`saturated_no_equality`); the inequality alone bounds the deep regime, `v_c⁴ ≤ a_M GM` (`deep_bound`, `deep_regime_upper_bound`), and the equality reached in the limit, `∫₀^r ε²A dr′/V_M(r) → 1`, gives `v_c⁴ = a_M GM` exactly (`mean_tendsto`, `deep_regime_equality`), nothing being assumed about the strain at any finite radius; `elastic`, `apparent_mass` and `tully_fisher` are the algebra at a radius where `ε²A = dV_M/dr`. The transition between Newton's law and the deep regime is not derived, in the paper or here. The field with the boundary condition is the pull less the push (`push`); the zero-gravity radius lies below the crossing for any mass below `c²R_Λ/π³G` (`zero_gravity`, `zero_gravity_mass`), and the push meets the deep pull at `r = R_Λv_c/c` (`crossover`, `crossover_speed`). At the horizon the Misner–Sharp energy is `E_Λ` and the Komar energy is `−2E_Λ` (`komar_misner_sharp`). The drift of `G` that a Hubble-radius horizon would give, `Ġ = 2H(1 + q)G`, is computed by differentiation (`G_drift`); with `L` fixed, `GΛ` is constant and `Λ̇/Λ = −Ġ/G` (`constancy_of_Lambda`); and the fluid equation, `ρ̇ = −3H(1 + w)ρ`, follows from the first law (`fluid_equation`). Since the ring's density `3π²ρ_c ln 2/2L²` does not change, it gives `w = −1` exactly (`w_exact`).

Some hypotheses are not inputs. `hdS`, that the horizon of the solution without a mass is at `R_Λ`, is derived by `horizon_radius` (Sec. IV.D): a ring is a closed geodesic of space, and of the maximally symmetric solutions without a mass only de Sitter space has one, so `Λ > 0` (the hypothesis `hΛ`); its closed geodesics are circles of radius `√(3/Λ)`, all of length `2π√(3/Λ)`, a classical property of the solution taken as known like the solution itself, and a largest ring, of length `Lℓ_c`, is one of them (`hgeo`, `Lℓ_c = 2π√(3/Λ)`); the theorems that use `Λ` keep `hdS` as their hypothesis for brevity. A few theorems take the condition that defines the quantity they treat: the proper distance, with `s(0) = 0` and `ds/dr = 1/√f` (`proper_distance`; `proper_distance_exists` shows that `R_Λ arcsin(r/R_Λ)` meets it); the shortfall of a sphere's area, by its rate per unit of geodesic distance (`shortfall_removes`); the vanishing of the field at `r_z` (`zero_gravity`); the balance of the deep pull and the push (`crossover`); the flat rotation curve, `r g_D → v_c²` (`deep_regime_upper_bound`); a state in which every joint outcome of the other qubits occurs, with the last qubit's bit given it at a probability strictly between 0 and 1, a random state or a product state of superposed qubits (`ceiling`, `ceiling_by_outcome`); and the observed bound on `|ρ̇/ρ|` (`w_bound`). `temperature_led` takes as its premise the alternative that Sec. IV.C rejects, that gravity follows the excess of the Deser–Levin temperature over the floor, and derives the coefficient that alternative would give.

## What was checked, and what it found

Every relation of the chain holds exactly as v11.2 writes it, and every number it computes is certified: each theorem gives an interval every point of which rounds to the value the paper quotes. Observational values and their uncertainties are inputs, not claims. Checking the draft of v11.2 found one slip. Appendix A gave `log₂ L_cos = 204.77`, which is the logarithm of `L_cos` after rounding it to `4.37×10⁶¹`. The logarithm of `L_cos` itself is `204.7648`, and `log2_Lcos` certifies `204.755 < log₂ L_cos < 204.765`, so v11.2 says `204.76`.

The ceiling theorems are stated for `L_cos`, the de Sitter horizon's ring length, which is the register's (Sec. IV.A); the register's rings are the largest rings whatever mass a horizon holds, and a mass at rest inside a horizon shortens the closed geodesics at its throat, `L_M = L − 2α/ln 2`, not the register's rings. The count of Sec. II is that of the horizon of the solution without a mass: the matter of a ball of radius `R_Λ` at today's mean density has `GM/c²R_Λ = Ω_m/2Ω_Λ = 0.23`, beyond the Nariai limit `1/3√3 = 0.19` up to which the solution has a horizon, a property of the solution taken as known and not derived here (`cosmic_mass_beyond_nariai` certifies the two numbers; `sds_outer_horizon` uses the weaker sufficient condition `13GM/c² < R_Λ`), so it is not a mass at rest inside a static horizon and Eq. (SM) does not apply to it.

Since v11.1, the formalization follows the paper's move to one bit per ring: the entropy is `(L²/4) ln 2`, `ln 2` is a field of `Reg`, and every formula that carries it (`η`, `G`, `ℓ_c`, the shift of the horizon, the crossing, `V_0`, the slope of `V_M`, the apparent mass, `GΛ`, `E_Λ` and `ρ_Λ`) is derived again and its numbers certified again. `π` and `ln 2` are now Mathlib's, with Mathlib's own bounds, where v11.1 took the bounds on `π` as a hypothesis, and the projective plane uses Mathlib's `Combinatorics.Configuration` in place of an extract of it. New in v11.2 are the bridges that join the count to the chain (the exact counts and Eq. (volume)), the shortfall summed into the entropy a mass removes, the paper's construction of a ring that misses a cell, the evenness of `L` from the opposite lying halfway round, the exact count behind the fraction `4n/L` and the count at each distance, the capacity count and its soft form, Palmer's bit count for every `N`, the proper distance to the horizon, the outer horizon with a mass, the deep-regime bound, the temperature floor with Deser–Levin, the Misner–Sharp and Komar energies, the coefficient of Einstein's equation, one bit as the entropy of a doublet, the ratio of the Planck density to `ρ_Λ`, `Λ̇/Λ = −Ġ/G`, and the numbers that go with them. The zero-velocity radii of v11.1 (111 pc and 1.6 Mpc) are gone, as the paper no longer quotes them, and so are the checks of two v11 misprints that v11.1 corrected.
